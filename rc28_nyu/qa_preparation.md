# Q&A Preparation

## How to use this file

Do not memorize these answers word for word. Memorize the first sentence of
each answer and the evidence that supports it. That is enough to produce a
clear 20–30 second response, even under pressure.

Use the same structure each time:

1. Answer the question directly.
2. Name the relevant comparison or robustness check.
3. State the limit of what the evidence establishes.

## 1. Is this simply a target-status gradient?

**Anchor:** No. Target status is the first layer, but the relational result
survives when the target itself is held fixed.

Panel A compares targets around the same source and skill. Panel B compares
sources around the same target and skill. The same domain reversal appears in
both. This does not prove direct transmission, but it shows that the result is
not reducible to a general propensity of high-status targets to change.

## 2. Why do you estimate source and target fixed effects separately?

**Anchor:** Because the signed gap is exactly target status minus source
status.

Including unrestricted source and target fixed effects simultaneously would
absorb the linear gap. The two panels therefore provide complementary
identification: one uses variation across targets around fixed sources, and
the other variation across sources around fixed targets. Agreement in signs
is the substantive test.

## 3. Does the analysis demonstrate diffusion between occupations?

**Anchor:** No. It identifies directional association in occupational skill
change, not observed pairwise transmission.

The source defines a current holder and the target defines the occupation that
changes. The design shows that change is systematically ordered by their
relative status, conditional on task-profile distance and fixed effects.
Mechanisms such as learning, closure, or task allocation remain
interpretations consistent with the pattern.

## 4. Could RCA denominator drift manufacture adoption and abandonment?

**Anchor:** We address that directly by freezing the RCA denominator at its
2015 composition.

The main directional pattern is robust to that construction, and raw
importance is used as an additional diagnostic. RCA removes uniform drift;
the checks are meant to assess whether compositional changes are driving the
result. I would still describe this as measurement sensitivity, not proof that
O*NET measurement is error-free.

## 5. Could skills with many source occupations dominate the triadic data?

**Anchor:** The result is not driven by source multiplicity.

We reweight each target-skill event by the inverse number of eligible sources.
All 24 signed-gap slopes preserve their sign; the mean magnitude change is
3.7 percent and the maximum is 11.8 percent.

## 6. Why is the projection figure informative if it uses the estimation sample?

**Anchor:** It is an internal sufficiency test, not validation.

The informative comparison is not observed versus fitted by itself. It is
that the signed-gap model reconstructs the aggregate gradient much better
than the symmetric status and distance-only nulls on the same risk set. The
figure isolates which component of the specification contains the relevant
information.

## 7. Why use a linear signed gap?

**Anchor:** The linear specification gives the most transparent test of
direction.

Its sign has a direct interpretation: whether change is associated with a
target above or below the source. The analysis also checks threshold and
alternative constructions in the SI, but the main argument does not require a
discontinuity or separate upward and downward slopes.

## 8. Are the three skill classes imposed on the data?

**Anchor:** They combine a data-driven functional partition with a distinction
in skill specificity.

The co-specialization network separates socio-cognitive from
sensory-physical content, and nestedness distinguishes general from
specialized socio-cognitive skills. The SI examines sensitivity to
classification choices. The taxonomy is a measurement strategy, not a claim
that every individual skill has only one possible meaning.

## 9. How should we interpret the similarity between some descriptive and model-based curves?

**Anchor:** Similar description does not make the triadic comparison
redundant.

The descriptive curves average over sources and therefore mix target
composition with relative position. The model asks a sharper question:
whether the same target changes differently depending on the status of
particular source occupations, and vice versa. Panel B is especially useful
because target status is fixed.

## 10. What is the strongest limitation?

**Anchor:** The strongest limitation is that the design is observational and
occupation-level.

We do not observe firms, workers, or direct channels of transmission.
Therefore the contribution is to document a robust relational pattern in
realized occupational content, not to identify a unique organizational
mechanism or an individual causal effect.

## Emergency short answers

- **What is the paper in one sentence?** Occupational skill change is
  directionally sorted by the relative status of source and target, with
  opposite patterns for socio-cognitive and sensory-physical content.
- **What is new?** The paper moves from asking which occupations change to
  asking how change is organized relative to the occupations that already
  hold each skill.
- **What is the key design feature?** Complementary source-fixed and
  target-fixed comparisons of a signed status gap, net of task-profile
  distance.
- **What should I remember if I freeze?** Target status is the first layer;
  Panel B shows the relational layer.
