Heterogeneous Diffusion Dynamics

Introduction: Diffusion as a Mechanism Generating Stratification

What is Diffusion?

  • Diffusion is the process by which ideas, behaviors, or innovations spread across a social network.

  • It transforms individual interactions into broader patterns of inequality through:

    • Network structures: Arrangements that create unequal access to resources
    • Relational dynamics: Connection qualities that determine how influence flows
    • Threshold mechanisms: Individual decision rules that trigger behavioral change

Diffusion as a Stratification Process

Diffusion generates inequality by:

  • Converting small initial differences into substantial gaps through cumulative advantage
  • Creating boundaries that limit access to valuable resources
  • Producing status hierarchies that persist beyond functional differences

Research Context and Analytical Framework

  • Current Limitations
  • Analytical Approach

Current approaches to diffusion and stratification are limited by:

  • Homogeneity assumptions: Models that treat all actors and influence processes as uniform
  • Single-dimension analysis: Focus on one type of relationship, ignoring how multiple ties interact
  • Static frameworks: Treatment of networks as fixed rather than evolving structures
  • Weak micro-macro connections: Insufficient explanation of how individual interactions create broader patterns

This research implements a systematic framework to identify diffusion mechanisms:

  1. Map heterogeneous actors in social space to identify different interaction patterns
  2. Track changes in social environments over time to capture dynamic processes
  3. Estimate counterfactual behaviors to separate selection from influence
  4. Measure direct effects on stratification outcomes
  5. Capture sequential dynamics of how actions change conditions for others
  6. Validate through empirically calibrated simulations

Core Theoretical Mechanisms

  • Transmission Pathways
  • Multiplexity & Bridges
  • Feedback Dynamics
  • Social influence operates through structurally different channels rather than uniform processes
  • Adoption thresholds vary systematically based on network position
  • Differential access to network positions creates unequal exposure to innovations and resources
  • Multiplexity: The overlap of different relation types (trust, resources, cooperation) creates robust diffusion channels
  • Wide bridges: Connections between groups that involve multiple relationship types, enabling complex practices to spread
  • Optimal consolidation: Moderate overlap between different social parameters creates the most effective diffusion structures
  • Initial structural advantages become self-reinforcing as diffusion outcomes reshape interaction opportunities
  • Network structures evolve in response to diffusion, often amplifying initial inequalities
  • These feedback loops explain how temporary advantages become persistent stratification

Structural Requirements for Different Diffusion Types

  • Content Requirements
  • Structural Conditions

Different types of content require distinct network structures:

  • Simple information: Spreads easily through weak ties and requires minimal reinforcement
  • Complex practices: Require multiple reinforcing pathways and demonstration opportunities
  • High-risk behaviors: Need strong trust relationships and redundant validation
  • Clustering: Dense connections within groups enable complex practice adoption
  • Bridge width: Multiple connections between groups facilitate cross-boundary diffusion
  • Parameter overlap: Moderate intersection of different social dimensions (status, discipline, occupation) creates optimal diffusion conditions

This explains why structurally different networks exhibit dramatically different diffusion outcomes despite similar connectivity levels.

Studies and progress

Study 1: Labor Market Stratification

Research Question and Objectives

  • Question: How do skill requirements diffuse across occupational networks to reinforce stratification?
  • Objectives:
    • Identify mechanisms of skill-based closure that maintain occupational boundaries
    • Analyze how symbolic devaluation processes affect skills in lower-status occupations
    • Model the interdependent dynamics of skill diffusion across occupational categories

Study 1: Labor Market Stratification II

  • Value Displacement
  • Research Progress
  • Skill clustering: Similar skills spread within occupational status groups, reinforcing boundaries
  • Differential valuation: Identical skills receive different economic rewards based on their location in the occupational network
  • Conceptual bridges: Skills transfer between occupations through overlapping skill categories that provide legitimation

Progress: 70% Complete

Key Findings:

  • 24% increase in skill-cluster separation over time, indicating strengthening occupational boundaries
  • 18-22% wage penalty for identical skills when located in lower-status occupations
  • Skills are 37% less likely to be adopted in lower-prestige occupations, controlling for functional relevance

Current work:

  • Testing alternative explanations through counterfactual simulations
  • Decomposition models isolating wage effects for structurally identical skills across occupational boundaries

Study 2: Social Movement Mobilization

Research Question and Objectives

  • Question: How do overlapping social ties enable high-risk protest diffusion under repression?

  • Objectives:

    • Develop a theory of strategic multiplexity explaining differential mobilization
    • Identify mechanisms that reduce participation thresholds for high-risk action
    • Model the spatiotemporal dynamics of protest diffusion across network configurations

Study 2: Social Movement Mobilization II

  • Strategic Multiplexity
  • Research Progress
  • Multiple confirming signals: Overlapping ties provide independent verification, reducing uncertainty
  • Capacity reinforcement: Combined resource and cooperation ties transform potential into action
  • Embedded commitment: Trust and cooperation ties convert personal obligations into political commitments

Progress: 85% Complete

Key Findings:

  • Participation thresholds reduced by 18-25% in networks with overlapping trust-resource ties
  • Cooperation-trust overlap strongly predicts high-risk activism (β=6.46)
  • Multiplex models predict actual diffusion patterns more accurately than single-dimension alternatives

Current phase:

  • Final manuscript preparation and historical case validation
  • Validation through 12 in-depth interviews with movement participants

Cross-Domain Integration: Unified Theory of Heterogeneous Diffusion

  • Common Mechanisms
  • Theoretical Synthesis

Three fundamental mechanisms operate across labor markets, social movements, and scientific communities:

  1. Structural opportunity effects: Initial positions constrain exposure to innovations, with constraints amplified through feedback processes

  2. Multiplexity and bridge formation: Overlapping relationship types create robust diffusion pathways across otherwise separated network regions

  3. Parameter interaction effects: The alignment or crosscutting of different social dimensions (status, discipline, occupation) systematically conditions diffusion processes

Progress: 65% Complete

  • Developing formal models linking micro-interactions to emergent stratification patterns
  • Specifying how these mechanisms generate similar outcomes across different empirical contexts
  • Identifying intervention points where structural modifications could reduce inequality

Expected completion: Q4 2025

Implications for Inequality and Social Integration

  • Structural Foundations
  • Practical Applications
  • Future Directions
  • Parameter consolidation: When social dimensions align perfectly (e.g., race, income, education), they create impermeable boundaries
  • Bridge formation: Strategic connections between different groups can facilitate resource diffusion
  • Network evolution: How networks change in response to diffusion often reinforces initial advantages
  • Institutional design: Create optimal levels of parameter overlap to facilitate integration
  • Strategic interventions: Build multiplex bridges between marginalized and resource-rich groups
  • Policy implications: Focus on structural conditions rather than individual attributes
  • Extend analysis to education, health, and economic mobility
  • Develop methods to measure and modify bridge structures in real-world networks
  • Examine how digital technologies transform traditional diffusion constraints
