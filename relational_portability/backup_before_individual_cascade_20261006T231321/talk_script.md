# Speaker script

Conceptual genealogy integrated before the contribution and formalization. Based on the updated main paper, SI and bibliography in build/overleaf_ready/.


## Who opens a community—and who moves it?

Open with the two questions, not a literature inventory. Diffusion joins successive accomplishments: an opportunity must arrive, and people must decide to act. We ask whether the same relational position remains valuable as the task changes.


## An opening matters through what follows

Rule, Sabetta and Bearman ask how events are tied into subsequent streams of action. Their argument concerns chance, pathways and the structures linking events, not diffusion or multiplex capacities. Our adaptation shifts attention from the origin of an opening to the connections that make it consequential. Entry, informational reach and costly commitment are successive accomplishments with different demands. This is a framing of our process, not a claim that their article derives our portability theory. Source: https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/download/23641/21216?inline=1


## A change of task can change which ties matter

Use the schoolteacher and the frequently visited family as the paper’s hypothetical example. Neither is an observed case. Advice can also discourage adoption and visiting can also carry information; relations are not exclusive channels. The question is which bundle of capacities the task draws on.


## Portability is continuity of relational advantage

Tasks are specified by what must happen and by the criterion of advantage before inspecting rankings. The focal actor need not perform the task: organizational designation and household adoption have different loci of action. Portability concerns relative advantage. Relations can support multiple capacities.


## Influence is specific; relations enable particular actions

This slide states premises, not claims of our invention. Katz and Lazarsfeld establish topical specificity of opinion leadership. Coleman defines social capital with reference to particular action; Gould demonstrates that formal and neighborhood networks did different work in mobilization; White grounds relations in identities and expectations. Our synthesis treats domains as infrastructures providing unequal bundles of capacities. Domains may support several capacities; no one-to-one mapping of relation and function is assumed. Source: main paper introduction and Relations Supply Different Capacities.


## Successive tasks can require different resources

McAdam compares Freedom Summer participants and withdrawals among applicants. The inference is that applying and consequential commitment are distinguishable accomplishments, not that all diffusion follows the same sequence. Cai et al. supports distinguishing information and learning; it does not derive portability. Rule et al. concerns chance and pathways; our application to diffusion is a conceptual adaptation. Together these readings motivate specifying tasks by what must happen for a process to advance, before observing any ranking differences. Source: main paper introduction and theory section.


## Does relational advantage survive the next task?

This is the bridge from inherited premises to the paper’s contribution. The argument moves from heterogeneity among relations and contextual value of prominence to continuity across tasks as the object of explanation. Do not claim that all prior network scholarship ignored task specificity or that the paper invented action-specific social capital. We formalize three jointly necessary conditions for ordinal reordering, distinguish score changes from rank changes, and then examine whether reordering creates collective reuse losses. Hsiao & Christakis 2026 and Becker et al. 2020 provide additional connections in appendix A1. Source: main paper introduction and portability proposition.


## We explain when relational advantage carries forward

The contribution is the conditional continuity of relational advantage across tasks within a process. Multiplex work motivates attention to different relations; the proposition specifies when a shift of task can reorder actors and derives exact null cases. The capacities are theoretical objects, not directly observed flows. The empirical tests examine implications rather than identifying every element of the mechanism.


## Advantage is a match between position and task

Omega expresses capacity–demand matching. R is a theoretical score, not an observed outcome or regression coefficient. BSS associations are not estimates of the capacity weights.


## Nonportability requires three conditions

Read the figure from A to C. Nonportability needs all three conditions together. Equivalent capacities, unchanged task demands, or identical positional profiles remove the basis for reordering. A changed score need not change a ranking: the appendix gives the exact pairwise condition and identity.


## The three conditions work jointly

Exact identity in the symmetric two-domain parameterization. A score change does not guarantee a rank reversal. A shift common to every actor can leave all orderings unchanged; pairwise profile differences are required for reordering.


## Positions can differ; advantage can reverse

The three conditions allow nonportability; they do not mechanically establish H2 in every empirical setting. The substantive bridge is that organizational entry privileges consultative prominence while costly household adoption may require observation, reassurance and coordination. Name generators do not prove that bridge. State the prediction before the evidence. The empirical contrast compares domain coefficients across the same households and requires actual opposite within-task gaps for the observed reversal.


## Pooling can hide a task-specific advantage

This prediction requires the setting-specific premise that the other pooled domains carry little adoption signal. Union centrality is calculated from union topology, not averaged centralities. The empirical comparison uses identical samples and specifications and directly tests the coefficient difference.


## Two datasets address different tasks

BSS networks and census were fielded in 2006 before entry in 2007–2010. The RCT is Chandrasekhar et al. 2026, not a stage of the BSS rollout. BSS units are households and the RCT unit is a village seed set. The two datasets do not follow the same people from information receipt to adoption; coefficients cannot be pooled or subtracted.


## Networks precede the arrival of microcredit

BSS invited teachers, shopkeepers and savings-group leaders. The designated pool is not a record of which households were actually contacted. Group microcredit entails repayment obligations, joint liability, default risk and public exposure. The network measure precedes adoption, preventing reverse causation from adoption to measured position but not eliminating confounding.


## Nominations become comparable household networks

Verified against scripts/02_networks.R: binary union of paired generators; symmetrization accepts either direction and diagonal is zero. Main-paper canonical diffusion centrality uses q=1/lambda_max and horizon equal to layer diameter, including t=0. Scores are min–max scaled on the village roster. Only sampled households answered the survey, so send-nomination opportunities differ: mention survey participation controls and measurement checks. Capacities are plausible interpretations, not measured flows.


## Three focal domains capture contrasting capacities

The paper follows the original paired-generator construction. These three domains give the main comparison a theoretically legible contrast between consultative, material and routine-interaction capacities. This is an interpretation of their analytic role, not a documented outcome-blind selection protocol. The data contain additional relations, audited in SI Section S4.4 and the table on additional BSS layers. The three-domain model is a focal specification, not an exhaustive ontology or a claim that only Visiting can matter. One position measure is used throughout: diffusion centrality normalized within village and layer. Do not justify the selection by exact harmonization with the RCT: money lending and borrowing have no direct counterpart there.


## Random seeding isolates the information benchmark

The RCT holds each village network fixed while random assignment varies seed position. Standardizing relative to the randomization distribution removes cross-village size and topology variation not randomized by assignment. Advice here is advice alone; BSS combines advice and decision support. RCT Material Exchange is not the same money-lending/borrowing generator pair. Precision depends on scaling and outcome; do not treat it as full identification of sequential portability.


## The studies answer different parts of the argument

The BSS comparison does observe designation and adoption for the same households. It does not observe information receipt and adoption as successive outcomes. The RCT is not a first stage of BSS. Neither dataset observes the information and adoption tasks for the same households. This distinction corrects the ambiguous wording in the earlier presentation, which could imply that the primary BSS outcomes were not observed jointly.


## Entry and adoption use identical comparisons

These are positions associated with outcomes, not causal effects of centrality. The models include village fixed effects and identical controls. Joint estimation preserves the covariance between the two outcomes observed for the same households. With 49 villages, we supplement clustered inference with a null-imposed wild-cluster bootstrap. Different base rates affect magnitude comparisons, but not the opposite signs of the two domain contrasts.


## Entry tracks advice; adoption tracks visiting

Preview the central finding, then explain why it is a test of portability. Designation and adoption are both outcomes of the focal household. This is not evidence that designated households caused neighbors to adopt. Networks were measured before the program entered.


## H1 · Households rank differently across domains

Related but not interchangeable. The third condition is met only partly; the domains are far from independent, limiting the potential for reordering. The top-decile figure describes the median village. The 21.8 percent statistic concerns household shifts between the two rankings, not the share of pairs reversing.


## H2 · Entry and adoption favor different domains

Panel A shows Advice/Decision associated with designation and little designation signal in the other domains. Panel B shows Visiting associated with own adoption and no detectable association for Advice/Decision or Exchange. Coefficients compare the least and most central household within a village. They are not causal effects. The Advice/Decision coefficient falls by .252 and Visiting rises by .200 between outcomes.


## The reversal is a tested contrast

Theta is (beta Visiting adoption minus beta Advice/Decision adoption) minus the same difference for designation. We test the domain contrast across outcomes, retaining within-household covariance. A nonzero Theta alone need not imply opposite rankings; here the two within-task gaps actually have opposite signs. A zero Theta means equal coefficient gaps, not proof that all actor rankings coincide. The 4.3 pp interquartile association gives a more realistic substantive magnitude than the full 0–1 coefficient.


## The Visiting association survives key checks

Do not describe the 49 leave-one-out estimates or the .021 bootstrap as tests of Theta: they concern the Visiting adoption coefficient. All eight constituent-generator versions of Theta are positive, and seven have p<.05. All sixteen Visiting-minus-Advice/Decision adoption contrasts across four scales and four samples are positive. In the 43 original villages, Visiting is .186, p=.011. Adjustment for designation yields .174.


## Visiting survives the inclusion of other relations

Each supplementary specification retains the three focal domains and adds the relation or bundle under examination, with union degree, wealth, entry-pool membership and village fixed effects. It is not one omnibus model simultaneously containing all additional layers. Rice/kerosene inflow and outflow enter the same model. Visiting remains positive and significant in every specification; other added layers have imprecise associations except the positive inflow result. This supports robustness of Visiting to the broader instrument, not its exclusivity. Kin visiting alone is null; splitting Visiting into kin, nonkin and unclassified partitions spreads the association without making each individually precise.


## H3 · Pooling ties conceals the adoption signal

This is a coefficient difference, not merely a contrast in statistical significance. These are the single-measure specifications; .156 should not be confused with .173 from the three-domain model. In the model including the three domains and the union, Visiting is .234 and the union -.204; their difference is .438, p=.021. Do not overinterpret the negative union coefficient given mechanical overlap with union degree. The result differs from Banerjee et al.’s village-level seeding finding and does not contradict it.


## The Visiting signal is concentrated in nearby ties

This is the strongest qualification. We partition Visiting edges at a distance of five enumeration positions and compute centrality separately for each partition. Both enter the joint adoption model. A nonsignificant far coefficient alone does not establish a statistically significant near–far difference. Household coordinates linkable to these records are unavailable; the proxy cannot separate learning from geographic exposure. Latent homophily remains possible.


## Advice favors information reach in the RCT

Random assignment varies seed-set position with the village network held fixed. Exposure is standardized relative to its within-village randomization distribution, removing differences in size and topology not generated by assignment. The direction is stable but precision depends on scaling and outcome. Treat this as supporting the informational side, not establishing the full cross-task claim. Advice here is the advice generator alone, while BSS uses an Advice/Decision composite; domains are harmonized rather than identical.


## A changed score need not reorder actors

This is an algebraic decomposition of the theoretical score, not a statistical decomposition of BSS outcomes. For actors i and j the score gap is Delta m + s tau Delta d. Reversal occurs iff the gaps at the two tasks have opposite signs. A common shift changes scores but not ranks. The exact pairwise condition is retained in appendix A4.


## Reordering and collective loss are distinct outcomes

Panel B uses q_i^1 q_j^1 weights, not observed eligibility or a deterministic filter. Panel C compares attainable coverage under the declared search procedure. The simulation is a scope exercise, not an empirical estimate of BSS losses. Different initiator sets can reach similar actors.


## The model changes task weights over fixed layers

Synthetic networks have group structure and heterogeneous activity. Correspondence is varied by relabeling the second layer, preserving its topology. The algorithm is greedy selection plus exchanges, with task-2 searches initialized from both a greedy set and the task-1 set: no claim of a global optimum in baseline networks. Exhaustive enumeration validates smaller networks. Three exact null conditions produce zero reversal and loss; sensitivity checks vary horizon, attenuation, exposure scale, budget and structure.


## Task change can reorder actors

Panel A counts all actor pairs with opposite task-specific orderings. The endpoints correspond to cross-layer correspondence .952 and −.016. All three null cases give zero. A rank reversal is an ordinal property, not adoption or a causal effect. Points average 100 network realizations. The figure and model are statements about scope, not empirical tests.


## Reordering persists among actors reached at task 1

Panel B weights each pair (i,j) by q_i^1 q_j^1, where q_i^1 is the modeled probability of reaching i with the task-1 initiator set. This is an eligibility-weighted share of reversals, not a separate experiment, a new observed BSS statistic or simply the count of households that actually adopted. It bridges a population-wide ranking to the sequential process: can the reordering affect actors likely to have passed the first task? Points average 100 network realizations. The figure and model are statements about scope, not empirical tests.


## Extensive reordering makes reuse costly

Panel C is coverage loss from reusing the task-1 initiator set instead of the task-2 set selected by the model’s search procedure. Modest reordering can be absorbed by redundant paths. Do not call this observed BSS loss or a causal adoption effect. The heuristic matches exact optima in small-network checks; there is no general proof of a global optimum for the baseline networks. Points average 100 network realizations. The figure and model are statements about scope, not empirical tests.


## Karnataka has high cross-domain correspondence

These are synthetic model predictions at the empirical correspondence, not observed BSS losses. The .776 correlation uses the model’s reach metric; .742 earlier uses diffusion centrality. They should not be presented as the same statistic. The observed-network exercise assumes regression associations are capacity weights: its 15.0% reuse loss and 21.4% reversing pairs are ceilings under the extreme task assignment and belong in the appendix. The two numerical exercises are not comparable estimates.


## What the evidence establishes—and leaves open

Name generators reveal infrastructure rather than content. The observational design leaves neighborhood exposure and latent homophily unresolved. Entry-pool Visiting-minus-Advice/Decision positions do not predict adoption among households within two steps: b=.002, p=.72. The secondary cross-village interaction is -.008, SE .182, p=.96; narrow variation in correspondence makes it uninformative. The RCT differs in units and does not observe BSS households. A direct mechanism test would measure tie-specific resources, information receipt and subsequent action for the same actors.


## Portability links relational structure to process

Return to the original question: those selected for entry need not be best positioned for household adoption. The contribution distinguishes functional specialization, changed task demands, and positional differentiation, then separates ordinal from collective consequences. BSS supports observed within-setting implications; RCT supports the informational benchmark; simulation establishes conditional collective scope. Capacities themselves are not identified empirically.


## Central in which relation—and for which task?

Close by resolving the opening question. In these villages the relation associated with advantage changed between entry and adoption. Invite discussion around how a direct design could observe information receipt, capacities and consequential action for the same actors.


## A1 · The contribution connects structural and multiplex work

We sit between two literatures. Structural work shows that returns to
targeting depend on tie organization. Multiplex work shows that relations are
not interchangeable. These works establish that relations and structure matter. We use those premises to make the continuity of advantage across successive tasks an explicit object of explanation, without making a blanket claim about all earlier scholarship.


## A2 · Diffusion centrality

This is expected transmission reach, not the count of unique people reached. Network sampling and measurement checks are reported in the paper. Mean domain degrees are 3.2, 4.2 and 5.3 for Advice/Decision, Exchange and Visiting; union mean degree is 7.0.


## A3 · Joint outcome models and the reversal contrast

Linear probability models. Domain scores range from zero to one within village; coefficients compare the least and most central household. A zero Theta denotes equal domain gaps, not proof of identical actor rankings. Opposite signs of the observed domain gaps establish the reversal independently of outcome base rates. Single coefficient p values generally use a normal reference, Wald contrasts a t reference with G−1 degrees of freedom. The null-imposed wild-cluster bootstrap for Visiting adoption yields p=.021.


## A4 · When do two actors exchange places?

The profile difference must be large enough to overcome common positional advantage. A change that shifts every actor’s score equally leaves rankings intact. The share of reversing pairs measures ordinal nonportability; eligibility-weighted reversal weights pairs by their probabilities of passing task 1. Reuse loss is the share of attainable task-2 coverage lost by using the task-1 optimal set instead of the task-2 optimal set.


## A5 · Checks on rival explanations

The design is observational. We address four alternatives before the results.
General prominence predicts that the same households lead in both tasks; it
does not predict that different domains order them for different tasks.
Spatial proximity is the one we cannot fully resolve: household coordinates
that can be linked to these records are not publicly available, so we use
enumeration order as a proxy.


## A6 · Composition and the secondary test

The spatial check is the most consequential qualification. At the most local
threshold, which classifies a third of Visiting ties as near, near ties
predict adoption and the remaining ties do not. This is what one would expect
if routine co-presence lets households observe how neighbors manage their
loans, and it is also what one would expect if neighborhoods differed in
exposure. We report the secondary test as uninformative. Positional
differentiation itself is established directly by H1; what the villages do not
provide is enough variation to show that the entry pool's advantage scales
with it.


## A8 · Simulation design and sensitivity

The network relevant for a task is a weighted combination of both layers, so no layer is intrinsically better. Varying correspondence by relabeling preserves layer structure. The selection heuristic matches exact enumeration in smaller networks. Sensitivity changes magnitudes but not the ordering. The model describes reach through infrastructure, not actual household adoption or interpersonal influence.


## A10 · Observed-network losses are ceilings

The paper treats this as an upper-bound exercise. A check of entry-pool positions against adoption within two steps yields b=.002, p=.72, giving no evidence of influence on neighbors. Part of the difference from the 6.8% synthetic-model loss plausibly reflects the selection budget.
